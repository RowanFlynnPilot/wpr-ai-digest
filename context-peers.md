# Who this is for

Rowan Flynn, technical lead at Wausau Pilot & Review (WPR), a small nonprofit local newsroom in Wausau, Wisconsin, and co-builder of the Old English Collective (OEC), which makes shared tools for local newsrooms — and WPR's editor, who decides what the newsroom tries next. Write so both can act on it: plain language, no code, with the editorial or audience angle stated as clearly as the technical one. This monthly issue watches what comparable newsrooms did — not what they covered: tools and trackers they built, products they launched, revenue and membership experiments, audience and community programs, reader call-outs, partnerships, notable hires, and AI policies or projects. The point is to learn from peers, borrow what works, and spot potential OEC partners or customers.

# What WPR has built (so "we already do this" and "we could copy this" are both clear)

Accountability archives (Care, Cleanup, Rent, Settlement, and Watch Ledgers; the OEC Ledger Framework), Gavel (civic meeting summaries and alerts), court/permit/property/Coming Soon trackers, Fire Watch (dispatch audio alerts), an obituary platform, election results and voter guide tools, an automated newsletter pipeline, a jobs board, contests, and reader tools like Fish Fry Finder. Revenue: sponsorships backed by Plausible analytics, newsletter placements, the jobs board.

# How to rank and pitch

Put one of these kinds first in the access field, then the newsroom:
1. **Built** — a tool, tracker, database, product, or feature a newsroom launched
2. **Revenue** — membership, events, sponsorship, philanthropy, or earned-revenue experiments
3. **Audience** — newsletters, call-outs, events, community programs, distribution experiments
4. **Newsroom** — hires and beats, partnerships, AI policies or projects, structural changes

Rank by how much WPR could learn from or copy it, with Wisconsin peers first when relevance is equal (they share WPR's readers, funders, and data sources). Say plainly whether WPR already does something similar. Applications should be concrete: what WPR would copy and how (an editorial or audience step, not only a technical one), or who at that newsroom is worth contacting (as the organization, e.g. "their audience team") — for a Ledger- or Gavel-shaped project, note the OEC partnership angle.

# Not worth surfacing

- Ordinary news coverage, even excellent coverage, unless the story is about how it was made (a published methodology, a data-sharing arrangement)
- Sponsored content and routine press releases from third parties
- Staff moves with no signal about strategy (a new beat or a new kind of role is a signal; a routine replacement is not)
- Anything already listed under "already covered"
