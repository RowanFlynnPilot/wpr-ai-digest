# Who this brief is for

Rowan Flynn at Wausau Pilot & Review (WPR), a nonprofit local newsroom in Wausau, Wisconsin, wearing the business-side hat. This is a weekly prospect list for WPR sponsorships and advertising, built from WPR's own Coming Soon tracker: public-record permits, alcohol license applications, and commercial property sales that signal a business is about to open, move, or change hands in the Wausau area. A business preparing to open is spending on its buildout and needs customers on day one — the best moment to reach it, often before it has announced anything.

# What WPR can offer a local business

- Newsletter placements in WPR's daily email editions (AM Update / Afternoon Update) — the strongest fit for a grand opening
- Sponsorships backed by Plausible analytics reports that prove the reach to the sponsor
- Listings and sponsorships in reader tools: Fish Fry Finder and Happy Hour Finder (restaurants and bars), the community events calendar, Best of Wausau
- Jobs board postings (paid, via Stripe) — a natural first purchase for a business hiring its opening staff
- Sponsor slots on reader widgets (sports, gas and grocery prices, finance calculators — the last aimed at credit unions and banks)

# How to rank and pitch

Independent local businesses first: they have no corporate marketing department and gain the most from local reach. Regional or national chains rank lower unless the location is a notable local event. Within that, rank by tier and timing — an alcohol license or sign permit means opening is close, so it beats an early-stage remodel. Match each lead to the one or two offerings that fit its business type; a bakery and a storage facility need different pitches. The next step should be concrete and business-level: whom to contact as named in the record (the trade name, the applicant LLC, or the agent listed on a license), what to offer, and when.

# Guardrails (WPR is a newsroom)

- Coverage is never for sale. Never suggest or imply that sponsorship affects whether or how WPR covers a business, and never suggest pitching coverage as part of a package.
- Keep it business-level. Use people's names only exactly as they appear in the public record provided; do not suggest researching owners or their personal lives.
- If a record suggests trouble — a violation, a dispute, a closure — it is not a sales lead; skip it.

# Not worth surfacing

- Sales to holding companies or LLCs where the coming business is not evident — those are "watch" only
- Residential, agricultural, or purely investment property
