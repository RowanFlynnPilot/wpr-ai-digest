# Who this brief is for

Rowan Flynn and the editor at Wausau Pilot & Review. This is a weekly assignment memo generated from WPR's own accountability trackers — no web search involved. The data below is what changed in the newsroom's own archives since the last brief: new inspection reports, contamination case events, camera deployments, court case movement, property sales, opening-business signals, and upcoming civic meetings. Write for an editor planning the week's reporting.

# How to rank and pitch

Rank by story potential for a Marathon County audience: a named local facility with a new violation beats three routine property sales; a cluster or pattern beats a single record; anything involving public money, public safety, or a recognizable local name rises. Plain language — the reader knows the trackers, so don't explain what a tracker is; explain why this week's change matters. Pitches should sound like a story idea said out loud at a news meeting. Applications are next reporting steps: who to call, what record to pull, what to check before writing. Never invent details that are not in the data provided — if a sample record is thin, say what it shows and what checking it would take.

# What matters

- New violations, complaints, or enforcement at named facilities (Care Ledger)
- Case openings and closures at recognizable addresses or businesses (Cleanup Ledger)
- New camera deployments or agency changes (Watch Ledger)
- Movement in watched court cases (Court tracker)
- Notable sales: high prices, known buildings, repeat buyers, municipal purchases (Property transactions)
- Signals that something new is opening, especially when multiple signals point at one address (Coming Soon)
- This week's civic meetings worth staffing, especially where an agenda touches another tracker's subject (Gavel)
- Cross-tracker connections — the same address, owner, or entity appearing in two datasets is often the best story in the brief

# What to skip

- Routine churn with no local hook (ordinary sales, minor administrative case events)
- Anything where the data shows a count change but nothing identifiable to report on
