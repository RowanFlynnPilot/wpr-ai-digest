# Who this is for

Rowan Flynn, technical lead at Wausau Pilot & Review (WPR), a nonprofit local newsroom in Wausau, Wisconsin, and co-builder of the Old English Collective (OEC). Every week several digests surface AI tools, techniques, journalism-industry news, Claude skills, practitioner ideas, and grants. This quarterly issue steps back from the week-to-week flow: it reads everything those digests surfaced in the quarter and says what it adds up to. Solo developer, builds with Claude Code; the live WPR builds are Ledgers (accountability archives), Gavel (civic meetings), court/permit/property/Coming Soon trackers, Fire Watch (dispatch audio), the obituary platform, election tools, the newsletter pipeline, and reader/revenue tools.

# How to rank and pitch

Each item is one of four kinds; put the kind first in the access field, then the evidence:

1. **Pattern** — a theme that recurred across weeks or editions (e.g. several picks about agents failing in production). Only call it a pattern if at least three picks support it; say how many and from which editions.
2. **Try next quarter** — the few highest-value experiments for WPR, drawn from the quarter's picks. Be selective: two or three strong ones beat six. Each must name the WPR build it would change and a concrete first step small enough to start in a week.
3. **Fading** — something that looked important early in the quarter and didn't hold up, or hype the picks themselves undercut. Skip this kind if nothing qualifies.
4. **Reader's taste** — if the feedback file shows votes or standing preferences, one item reflecting what they reveal and how the digests should adjust. Skip if there is no feedback yet.

Order: patterns first, then try-next-quarter, then fading, then reader's taste. Ground every item in named picks; a synthesis that could have been written without the list is a failure. Plain language, no hype. If the quarter's data is thin, say so in the first item rather than stretching it.
